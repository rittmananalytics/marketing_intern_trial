## Background: what we do

Most medium and large companies run their business on lots of separate software: a website, a payment system, a CRM, an email tool, a warehouse system. Each one holds a piece of the picture. None of them holds the whole picture.

Rittman Analytics is a nine-person consultancy in Brighton. Companies hire us to pull all that scattered data into one place and turn it into dashboards and reports the business can actually trust. Think of it as building the plumbing, then the taps.

This work has traditionally been slow. A typical project runs for months. Our clients find that frustrating, and so do we.

## Background: what Wire is

Over the past two years, AI tools have appeared that can write software. You give them an instruction in plain English and they produce working code. The two we use are called Claude Code and Gemini CLI.

These tools are fast. Left unsupervised, they are also unreliable in a specific and dangerous way. They produce code that runs without errors but is subtly wrong. In our line of work that means a dashboard that displays a confident number which happens to be incorrect, and a client who makes a decision based on it. That is worse than being slow.

**Wire is our answer to that problem.** It is a set of rules, templates and automated checks that sit around the AI tools and control how they work. The AI still does the writing. But at every stage it must follow our house method, pass a set of automated quality checks, and get sign-off from a human before it is allowed to move on to the next stage.

The result: work that used to take months now takes days, without losing the quality control.

The clearest proof is a project for Barton Peveril Sixth Form College. From contract signature to live dashboards with staff trained to use them took **eight working days**, for £7,100. A conventional project of that shape would take two to three months.

Wire is now a product in its own right. It has its own page on our website, its own public documentation, and ten different versions for different sizes of project.

## The Business Problem

Almost nobody knows Wire exists. That is what we need you to fix.

There are two obstacles that make this harder than promoting a standard consultancy service.

**1. Our audience is sceptical, and reasonably so.**

The people who make these buying decisions are heads of data, data directors and analytics managers. Many of them have already watched an AI tool produce plausible-looking work that turned out to be wrong. Some have cleaned up the mess personally. So the phrase "we use AI to go faster" does not excite them. It worries them.

Any campaign that leads with speed alone will bounce off this audience. The interesting marketing challenge here is making a sceptical, technical buyer believe that this particular approach is different, and giving them a reason to spend thirty minutes finding out.

**2. There are two possible buyers, and they want different things.**

- **Buyer A: a company that needs data work done.** They do not care how the work happens. They care that it arrives sooner and costs less. Wire is a reason to hire us rather than a competitor.
- **Buyer B: a company with its own data team that wants to work this way themselves.** They are not buying our people. They are buying the method, so their own team can adopt it.

These two groups respond to different messages and use different channels. Deciding which one to lead with, and saying why, is part of the task.

### What success looks like

Our goal is a booked **30-minute walk-through call**, where a prospect sees Wire demonstrated live on a real project. That call is the point at which our sales conversation properly begins. Everything before it exists to earn it.

### Your Task

Design a marketing campaign that promotes Wire using the digital channels we commonly use. The campaign should aim to:

1. **Raise awareness** that AI-assisted delivery only works when it is properly controlled, and that Wire is how we control it.
2. **Drive engagement** with the Wire page, the documentation and the Barton Peveril case study.
3. **Convert interest** into booked 30-minute walk-through calls.

### Deliverables

1. **Campaign Strategy**

  - Identify the **target audience**: job titles, industries, company sizes. State which of the two buyers above you are leading with and why.
  - Define the **core messaging**. This is the hardest and most important part. Your messaging needs to answer the sceptic: why should someone who distrusts AI-written work believe this is different?
  - Outline how you would use the available channels (LinkedIn, email, website) to reach this audience.

2. **Campaign Plan**

  - A practical plan covering channels, the content you would create, and how often you would post or send.
  - Include example **social media posts**, an outline **email sequence**, and any **landing page** suggestions. Rough drafts are fine. We want to see your thinking and your writing, not a finished asset.
  - Consider how you would use evidence. We have real numbers and a real named client. Work out where proof belongs in the sequence.

3. **Lead Conversion Tactics**

  - Explain how you would move someone from first seeing a post to booking a call. Follow-up emails, retargeting ads, automation, whatever you think fits.
    
4. **Industry Trends and Best Practices**

  - Show that you have looked into what is currently happening in B2B marketing and in AI tools for software teams. Use what you find to make the campaign feel timely rather than generic. Twenty minutes of reading is enough. We are looking for evidence of curiosity, not a literature review.

5. **Performance Metrics**

  - Propose the measures you would track. These might include engagement rates, traffic to the documentation, case study reads, calls booked, or how many booked calls turn into real opportunities.
  - For each one, say what number would tell you the campaign is working and what number would tell you to change course.

### Glossary

Terms you will meet on our website and in the documentation. You do not need to master these. This is here so nothing stops you.

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Data platform | The combined systems that collect a company's data, tidy it up and store it ready for reporting. The plumbing. |
| Dashboard | A screen of charts and numbers that a business user looks at to run their part of the business. |
| Data warehouse | The central database where all the tidied-up data lives. BigQuery and Snowflake are two popular ones. |
| Pipeline | The automated process that moves data from a source system into the warehouse on a schedule. |
| dbt | A widely used tool for tidying and reshaping data once it is in the warehouse. |
| Looker, LookML | Looker is a dashboard tool owned by Google. LookML is the language used to configure it. |
| Analytics engineer | The type of specialist who does this work. Our clients employ them. So do we. |
| Claude Code, Gemini CLI | AI tools that write software from plain-English instructions. Wire sits around these. |
| Coding agent | General term for an AI tool that writes and edits code with limited supervision. |
| SOW (Statement of Work) | The document that defines what a project will deliver, by when, for how much. The contract. |
| Autopilot | The Wire mode that takes a signed SOW and runs most of the project automatically, pausing for human approval at defined points. |
| Test coverage | The proportion of the work checked automatically for correctness. Higher is better. We quote 100% on the Barton Peveril project. |
